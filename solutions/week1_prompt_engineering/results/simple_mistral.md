 Photosynthesis is a vital process in which green plants, algae, and some bacteria convert carbon
 dioxide (CO2), water (H2O), and sunlight into glucose (a type of sugar) and oxygen (O2). This
 process takes place within the chloroplasts of plant cells. Here's a simplified breakdown of
 photosynthesis:

1. **Light-dependent reactions**: These occur in the thylakoid membranes of the chloroplasts and are
driven by sunlight. During this stage, water molecules (H2O) are split into oxygen gas (O2) and
high-energy electrons, protons, and ATP (adenosine triphosphate).

2. **Light-independent reactions** or Calvin cycle: These take place in the stroma of chloroplasts and
utilize the energy from ATP and the electrons generated during light-dependent reactions to convert
carbon dioxide (CO2) into glucose (C6H12O6). This process involves three main stages:
   - Carbon fixation: CO2 is combined with an organic compound called ribulose bisphosphate (RuBP),
     producing two molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate.
   - Reduction: The 3-phosphoglycerate molecules are converted into glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate,
     releasing a phosphate group in the process. This release is balanced by the regeneration of RuBP
     from the phosphate and other compounds.
   - Regeneration: The remaining glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate molecules are converted into glucose or
     stored for later use, while more RuBP is produced through a series of reactions.

The overall equation for photosynthesis can be represented as follows:
6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 (glucose) + 6 O2

This process not only provides plants with the food they need to grow but also releases oxygen, which
is essential for most forms of life on Earth.
